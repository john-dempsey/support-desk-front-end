From Casey's Click to Alex's Count

One comment, followed through two browsers and the API: saved, turned into a notification, pushed to Alex's socket, and counted again over REST.

Casey comments on ticket 1, which is assigned to Alex. Alex, logged in in another browser, sees "Notifications (3 unread)" become "(4 unread)" without touching anything. Here's every step in between:

Three programs, one click

  • Casey's browser (steps 1 and 2's request) is Stage 3's comment form, unchanged. It knows nothing about notifications.
  • The API (steps 2 to 4 and 7) is what you built in Lessons 5 and 8: a domain event, a listener, and notify() with its channels.
  • Alex's browser (steps 5, 6 and 8) is this stage: the socket from topic 04, and the count from topic 03.

Nothing in Alex's browser asked for anything until step 6. The push arrived on its own, over a connection that had been open since Alex logged in.

The push is a nudge

Step 5 carries the whole notification: its message, its ticket, its id. The provider ignores all of that. It only needs to know that something happened, and then asks the API, in step 6, what's true now. That's "REST is the truth, the socket is a nudge" (topic 01), written as code.

Try it

Log in as Alex in one browser and Casey in the other (one of them in a private window), side by side. Open the Network tab in Alex's window, with the Fetch/XHR filter on.

  1. As Casey, comment on ticket 1. Watch Alex's count, and the requests in Alex's Network tab. Which step of the diagram is each request?
  2. As Alex, assign one of the unassigned tickets to Alex. Does Alex's own count go up? Who did the API decide to tell, and is that what you'd expect?
