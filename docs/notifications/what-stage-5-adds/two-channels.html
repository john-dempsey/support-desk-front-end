Two Channels

Notifications reach the browser two ways: the saved history over REST, and a live push over a socket. Each one on its own would miss something.

The API tells the front-end about notifications in two different ways, and Stage 5 uses both:

Why not just one?

Only REST would work, but nothing would change on screen until the user reloaded the page or clicked a link. Asking the API every few seconds ("polling") would fix that by flooding it with requests, almost all of them answered "nothing new" (Lesson 5, "Real-Time Push with Socket.IO").

Only the socket would miss things. A push goes to whoever is connected at that moment, and nothing is replayed later. So anything that happens while the user is logged out, or while their laptop is closed, would never reach them. And SLA breaches would never arrive at all: they're found by the worker process (Lesson 6), which has no socket to push with.

REST is the truth, the socket is a nudge

So the saved rows are what the app believes. A push just means "there's something new, go and look". You'll see that idea in every part of this stage: when a push arrives, the unread count is asked for again over REST, not worked out from the push.

That's also why the stage is built in two parts:

  1. History over REST (topics 02 and 03). This works with no socket at all.
  2. Live updates over Socket.IO (topics 04 to 06), added on top.
