Not Every Effect Is a Fetch

AuthProvider and NotificationsProvider also have an ignore flag, but they aren't pages waiting for data, so they keep their own effects.

Topic 02 asked which two files with an ignore flag aren't pages. They are AuthProvider and NotificationsProvider. Both still have their own useEffect, and that's on purpose.

useFetch is for one situation: a page that waits for data, shows "Loading…" until it comes, then shows it or an error. The providers do something different:

A page with useFetch AuthProvider NotificationsProvider
When it asks when the page opens, or its argument changes once, when the app starts when user changes
Sometimes doesn't ask at all no no saved token: nothing to check nobody logged in
On a failure shows the error forgets the token: logged out keeps the last count, says nothing
Also connects the socket, listens for pushes
Who sees the result that page every component (a context) every component (a context)

None of those fit the hook. It always asks, always keeps an error for the page to show, and its state belongs to one component. Bending it to cover the providers would mean options and ifs for every difference, and a hook that's harder to read than the effects it replaced.

A custom hook is worth writing when the code really repeats. The five pages repeated the same thing exactly; the providers only look similar.

The pages keep some effects too

useFetch only replaced the effects that load data. The effects that listen for pushes, in the two ticket pages and the notifications page (Stage 5), are still there. They don't fetch anything: they register a handler and return the "stop listening" function.

Try it

Count the useEffect calls left in src/ (leaving out the tests):

grep -rn "useEffect(" src --include=*.jsx --include=*.js | grep -v test

You get seven lines. For each one, say in a few words what it does. Which one loads the data for every page now? (Two of the seven are in AuthProvider: what does the second one do?)
